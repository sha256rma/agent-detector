Agent detection

Human, agent, or bot?

Each player answers a few randomized word puzzles and the question “Are you an AI agent?”, and the detector uses their accuracy, their speed, and their answer to decide whether they are a human, an LLM agent, or a software bot.

What this detector can and can’t promise

We assume whoever is on the other side controls everything on their own computer. They choose the answers, they can wait as long as they like before answering, and if they run an AI model, they write its instructions. The only things we control are the puzzles and the clock on our server.

  1. Scripts that don’t understand words

    A script that picks words without understanding them gets about 1 in 5 puzzles right. With 6 puzzles, it has a 1.7% chance of getting enough right to look like it understands.

    This depends on every puzzle being new, and the script not having a copy of our word list.

  2. Answering faster than a person

    Only 5% of people answer a puzzle in under 1.20 s. A player who is regularly faster than that is very likely not a person. Being slower doesn’t show that someone is human, because a program can wait before it answers.

    This depends on answers not being prepared in advance. Each puzzle is used once, timed on our server, and expires after 60 seconds.

  3. Asking “Are you an AI agent?”

    If an agent answers this question honestly, we label it an agent, even if it answers the puzzles slowly on purpose to look human.

    This depends on the agent being allowed to tell the truth. The small model in this demo answered honestly every time we tested it, even when its instructions told it to say no.

  4. What we can’t catch

    An agent whose owner makes it answer “No” and also makes it wait before every answer. It looks exactly like a slow, careful person, so we label it human.

    To catch it we would need proof from the player’s device, such as hardware that confirms a real person is typing. You can try this case: choose “Stalling agent”, then “Its code ignores the model and says no”.

Does it understand the words?

Each puzzle shows four words from one category and one word from another, so a script that doesn’t understand meaning guesses right only one time in five.

Is it faster than a person?

The server times every answer with its own clock and compares the typical time to a threshold that only 5% of real people beat.

Does it admit to being an agent?

Before the first puzzle, every player is asked “Are you an AI agent?”, and today’s agents usually answer honestly, which catches agents that slow down on purpose.
